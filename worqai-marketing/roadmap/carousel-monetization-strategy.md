## SECTION 1: THE REAL OFFER

The strongest core offer is **white-label carousel production for agencies** — Kenneth is the invisible production engine behind their client deliverables. He delivers branded, on-spec, agency-quality 8-slide carousels in 24-48 hours at a fraction of their internal cost.

He is not selling carousels. He is selling **margin and capacity** to agency owners.

### Why this framing wins

1. **Agencies already have the demand.** They have signed clients who need monthly content. Kenneth doesn't have to create demand — he intercepts existing budget.
2. **Repeat revenue is automatic.** A small agency with 4 retainer clients needs 16-32 carousels/month. One closed agency = predictable monthly cash.
3. **No client-facing brand work.** Kenneth never has to deal with end-client revisions, brand guideline calls, or strategic feedback. The agency owns that layer.
4. **Speed is his moat.** Agencies bleed time on production. "I can give you 8 slides in 2 hours, on-brand, ready to post" is a closer line than any pitch a freelancer can make.
5. **It positions him as a peer, not a vendor.** White-label = partner. Freelance = expense. Different psychology, different price tolerance.

### Secondary offers (only if agency lane stalls in week 1)

- **Overflow production** for in-house marketing teams of mid-size LATAM brands.
- **Done-for-you monthly carousel pack** for solo agency owners and consultants who don't have a designer at all.

The order matters: white-label first, overflow second, DFY third. Do not lead with DFY — that is freelancer positioning and competes on the wrong axis.

## SECTION 3: FREE DEMO STRATEGY

### Is a free custom demo the right weapon?

Yes — but only as a **closing tool**, not a prospecting tool. Most agencies will not engage a cold DM with a portfolio link. They will engage with "I'll make you one with your real client, on your brand, in 1 hours, no charge — only if you'd actually use it."

The demo is the wedge that turns a "maybe" into a contract. It is not a lead magnet broadcast to the world.

### Who to offer it to (qualify first)

Only after these three boxes are checked in conversation:

1. They have **at least one active retainer client** producing social content monthly.
2. They have **expressed a real production pain** ("our designer is overloaded", "our turnaround is slow", "we're growing").
3. They have a **real Instagram** with recent posts (look at it — if dead, no demo).

If any of those three is missing, send the portfolio link instead. Custom demos cost real time. Time = oxygen right now.

### What exactly to send

- **Full carousel.** Up to 8 slides. 1080x1080. HD PNG ZIP delivered the same way a paying client would receive it.
- **Watermarked? No.** A watermarked demo is a portfolio piece, not a sample of the real product. The agency needs to feel "this is exactly what I'd hand off."
- **One small but visible signature** in the file metadata or a tiny brand mark on slide 8 — easily removed if they buy. This is for traceability, not protection.
- **File naming matters.** Name the file like a premium studio: `[NombreAgencia]_WhiteLabel_DarkPress.pdf` not `export_1234.pdf`. This subliminally signals a serious operation before they open it.

### Hard limits

Included in the free demo:
- One topic, one carousel.
- One round of light edits (typos, swap a word) within 24 hours.
- Delivered as ZIP of PNGs, plus the editable HTML.

NOT included:
- Strategy, copywriting from scratch (they provide the copy or the topic).
- Multiple format variations.
- Stories, Reels, video — only the static carousel.
- More than one round of revisions.

State the limits in the same DM that offers the demo. No surprises later.

### How to prevent abuse

1. **Qualify before producing.** Three boxes above. No exceptions.
2. **48-hour decision window after delivery.** "If you want to move forward, let me know within 48 hours. If not, no problem — I'll keep the file as a portfolio piece."
3. **Limit to 5 free demos per week, max.** After the first 1-2 close, drop to "demo by referral or paid trial only."
4. **Track who got one.** Spreadsheet with name, agency, date, outcome. No agency gets two free demos.
5. **The demo is on Kenneth's topic choice if they don't provide one.** Don't burn 4 hours on their bespoke brief for free.

### What makes the demo irresistible vs. a giveaway

Irresistible: it is **on their actual brand** with **their actual client's vibe** and **their handle**. It feels like it's already theirs.

Giveaway: a generic Profile Pro LATAM-style sample with no personalization that they can use without paying.

The personalization is the conversion driver. A cold sample does nothing. A "your brand, your client, ready to post on Tuesday" demo flips them in 24 hours.

### The single strongest demo format

> "Pick one of your current clients. Send me their handle and one topic from your content calendar. I'll deliver an 8-slide carousel on their brand within 1 hour. If it's the level you'd hand to that client, we talk pricing. If it's not, you keep the file."

That is the demo. Nothing else.

## SECTION 4: HOW TO UNDERCUT WITHOUT LOOKING CHEAP

### The pricing psychology

Agencies do not buy "cheap." They buy **margin**. The same $40 carousel can read as "junk freelancer" or "smart white-label partner" depending on framing.

Rules:
- Always quote **per deliverable**, never per hour. Hours signal commodity labor.
- Always quote in USD. Quoting in colones or pesos signals local-tier pricing.
- Always show the agency what they save, not what you charge.

### How much lower to go

Position at **40-55% below what an agency would pay a mid-tier freelance designer**, and **65-75% below what they'd bill the end client**.

| Reference price | Where to land | Why |
|------------------|---------------|-----|
| Freelance designer charges agency $80-120/carousel | Kenneth's wholesale: $35-55 | Clear margin win, doesn't trigger "too cheap" alarm |
| Agency bills end client $150-300/carousel | Agency keeps $100-250 margin | They will buy on math alone |
| In-house designer salary | Replace 1 designer's monthly carousel output for $400-700 | Same as a junior designer's monthly cost |

Going lower than 40% below freelance triggers suspicion. Going higher than 55% below kills the urgency.

### The language of savings

Do not say:
- "I'm cheaper than a freelancer."
- "Affordable carousels."
- "Budget-friendly."

Do say:
- "Frees up your designer for the work clients actually notice."
- "up to 8 slides carousels delivered in 1 hours — usually 6-10 hours of internal time."
- "Replaces ~$800/mo of design hours at $X/mo."
- "Same standard, half the cost, none of the back-and-forth."

Every quote should arrive next to a number that frames the alternative cost. Never bare.

### How to make it feel premium but obvious

Three signals:

1. **Process clarity.** "Brief in. Carousel out in a couple hours. One revision round. Delivered as ZIP + editable HTML." Premium services have systems. Cheap ones improvise.
2. **Confidence on price.** Quote once. Don't negotiate down on the first call. Discount only via volume bundles.
3. **Quality floor stated upfront.** "If the first carousel isn't on the level of the samples, you don't pay." Risk reversal looks expensive but it isn't — the system is good enough that this is safe.

## SECTION 8: DM POSITIONING

Kenneth is **not a freelancer**. He is **a production operator** running a system that produces agency-grade carousels at speed. The mental model the prospect should form: "this guy runs an in-house studio I rent."

### Two archetypes — one per audience

**Track A (Agencies): The Bottleneck Killer.**
The quiet operator who runs the back end of agencies without being on the website. Calm, numbers-first, production-focused. Talks about margin, capacity, and turnaround. Does not chase. Does not pitch. Identifies their pain and offers to solve it.

**Track B (Founders/Creators): The Premium Studio.**
A decisive creative operator who takes the design burden off founders entirely. Confident, direct, slightly authoritative ("publicalo esta semana"). Never mentions speed in seconds or minutes — sells status, autopilot, and consistency. Frame as "nuestro estudio" even if solo.

These are the opposite of:
- Hungry freelancer ("would love to work with you")
- Hype marketer ("REVOLUTIONARY AI carousels!")
- AI-bro ("manejemos esto en automático")
- Vague generalist ("I help brands grow on Instagram")

### What makes him different from the 50 others in their DMs

1. He is solving an **agency problem**, not pitching a creator service. Most DMs are creators trying to sell to brands. He is selling to agencies.
2. He shows **process**, not portfolio. Anyone can have nice samples. Few can ship 8 slides on-brand in 48h with a real system.
3. He **qualifies them**, instead of begging. The DM has filter language ("only if you have an active retainer").
4. He **mentions a specific client of theirs** when relevant. Personalization that takes 60 seconds.

### Specific language and tone

Use:
- "Production partner", "white-label backend", "overflow capacity", "design ops", "turnaround"
- Numbers: "8 slides in 48h", "$X per carousel", "12/mo"
- Direct verbs: "I run", "I produce", "I deliver", "I work with"

Avoid:
- "I'd love to..." "Hope you're well" "Just wanted to reach out"
- "We are a team of..." (implies fake size, agencies smell it)
- "Best in class", "premium quality" (empty)
- Emojis, exclamation marks, "!!"

Tone: like texting a peer. Lowercase if it fits the prospect's IG. Short sentences. No paragraphs longer than 3 lines. Always end with a clear, low-friction next step.
