## SECTION 4: APPLICATION HACKS & INTERVIEW TRICKS

### Application Optimization Hacks

1. **Keyword Stuffing (White Text Hack):**
   - Copy the job description's exact keywords
   - Paste them in white (invisible) text at the bottom of the resume PDF
   - ATS scanners read them, humans don't see them
   - WARNING: Some modern ATS systems detect this. Use sparingly on older ATS platforms.

2. **The 2-Minute Rule:**
   - Apply within 2 hours of a job being posted. Early applicants get 8x more interviews.
   - Set up alerts on LinkedIn, Indeed, and Glassdoor for target titles.

3. **The Recruiter DM Strategy (LinkedIn):**
   - After applying, find the recruiter or hiring manager on LinkedIn
   - Send a short, direct message: "Hi [Name], I just applied for the [Title] role. My background in enterprise service transitions and SLA governance at TD SYNNEX/Broadcom maps directly to this. Would love to connect."
   - Do NOT send a long message. Keep it under 3 sentences.

4. **The "I Already Started" Hack:**
   - In the cover letter or application note, reference something specific about the company's current challenges
   - Example: "I noticed [Company] is scaling its LATAM operations — I've managed exactly this type of multi-vendor, multi-region coordination at TD SYNNEX."
   - This shows initiative and makes you memorable.

5. **Apply on Company Career Pages Directly:**
   - Many companies prioritize direct applicants over job board applicants
   - Always find the company's career page and apply there IN ADDITION to the job board

6. **Tailor the First 3 Bullets:**
   - The first 3 bullet points of your resume must mirror the job description's top 3 requirements
   - Use Kenneth's resume-rewriting skill for this

7. **The Follow-Up Cadence:**
   - Day 0: Apply
   - Day 1: DM recruiter on LinkedIn
   - Day 5: Follow-up email if no response
   - Day 10: Second follow-up with a value-add (article, insight, or relevant case study)
   - Day 14: Move on if no response

8. **Salary Anchoring:**
   - Never give a number first. Say: "I'm flexible and want to understand the full compensation package. What's the range for this role?"
   - If forced: Anchor high. Say "$5,000-6,000/month" for regular roles. For contracts, say "$30-45/hr."
   - For Costa Rica-based roles at US companies, market rate for PM roles is $2,500-5,000/month.

9. **The Reference Hack:**
   - Prepare 3 references who will give you a 10/10 review
   - Brief them on what the company is looking for before they get called
   - Give the reference checker your strongest reference first

10. **Portfolio / Case Study:**
    - Create a 1-page "case study" PDF showing a project win (e.g., the 350+ store remodel coordination)
    - Attach it to applications or share in interviews
    - This differentiates you from 95% of applicants who only submit a resume

### Interview Preparation Protocol

When Kenneth has an interview coming up:

1. **Research the company:**
   - Glassdoor reviews (filter for role-specific reviews)
   - LinkedIn: company page, recent posts, hiring manager's profile
   - News: recent funding, layoffs, expansions, product launches
   - Crunchbase: funding stage, investors, employee count

2. **Prepare STAR stories (Situation, Task, Action, Result):**
   - Story 1: The 350+ store remodel coordination at TJX/TD SYNNEX
   - Story 2: The Broadcom service transition and Transition Command Center
   - Story 3: The ISO 27001 scope ownership and audit readiness
   - Story 4: The 3-Strike SLA protocol that reduced aged tickets
   - Story 5: Coinbase Tier-3 escalation resolution and SOP creation

3. **Prepare questions to ask:**
   - "What does success look like in this role at 30, 60, and 90 days?"
   - "What's the biggest operational challenge the team is facing right now?"
   - "How does this role interact with engineering and support teams?"
   - "What tools does the team currently use for project tracking?"

4. **Technical prep for PM interviews:**
   - Be ready to explain: Critical path method, RAID logs, SLA frameworks, Agile vs Waterfall trade-offs
   - Be ready to whiteboard or walk through a project timeline
   - Know your numbers: 350+ remodels, 60+ decommissions, Fortune 100, Tier-1 cybersecurity client

### Overemployment Operational Security (J2 Rules)

- **J2 MUST be a contractor/freelance role** — no W-2 or CCSS dual-reporting
- **Never mention J1 to J2 or vice versa**
- **Use separate devices if possible** (or at minimum separate browser profiles)
- **Decline video-on meetings when possible** for J2 — "camera issues"
- **Block calendar overlaps** — J1 meetings take priority, J2 must be flexible
- **Never add J2 to LinkedIn** — keep profile focused on J1/TD SYNNEX experience only
- **Use a separate email for J2 applications** if possible
- **Contractor payments via Deel, Payoneer, or direct wire** — avoids payroll system conflicts

## SECTION 9: RED FLAGS TO AVOID

- Companies that won't disclose salary range after first interview
- "Unlimited PTO" with no clear boundaries (often means no PTO)
- Roles requiring you to be an "independent contractor" but treating you like an employee (misclassification)
- Companies with less than 6 months of Glassdoor reviews
- Roles that require purchasing your own equipment with no stipend
- Any company that asks for payment as part of the hiring process (scam)
- Extremely fast offers with no technical assessment (could be unstable or scam)
