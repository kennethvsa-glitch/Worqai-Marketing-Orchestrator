# Anti-Generic Creative Policy

WorqAI uses AI to widen exploration, not to average every idea into the safest familiar answer. Semantic specificity comes before visual polish.

## Research basis

- Google Research (2026) found that convergent AI workflows can close ambiguity too early and cause “aesthetic sanitization”: polished but generic work that loses individual nuance. The recommended direction is productive tension, contextual memory, divergent exploration, and rigorous critique. Source: https://research.google/pubs/in-search-of-weird-corners-diagnosing-the-limits-of-convergent-ai-in-professional-creative-practice/
- A comparative study found that people using an LLM for ideation produced ideas that were less semantically distinct across users. More output and more detail do not prove originality. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01536
- Google Research reported that expressive design grounded in hierarchy and fundamentals helped participants fixate on the correct element 33% faster and complete tasks 20% faster than the prior system in its study. Expression must improve comprehension, not merely novelty. Source: https://research.google/pubs/usability-hasnt-peaked-exploring-how-expressive-design-overcomes-the-usability-plateau/
- Apple requires motion to support status, feedback, instruction, or understanding and warns against motion added for its own sake. Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion
- Atlassian recommends one clear focal event, semantic motion chosen by intent, and removing animation when its absence does not cost information or context. Source: https://atlassian.design/foundations/motion
- IBM Carbon separates productive motion from occasional expressive motion and requires related movements to share a coherent spatial relationship and hierarchy. Source: https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/motion/overview/
- Adobe’s 2026 research emphasizes relevance, genuine usefulness, cultural authenticity, and final human creative judgment; its Creators’ Toolkit survey reports that 85% of surveyed creators want the final creative decision to remain human. Sources: https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2026/01/08/how-creators-leveraging-adobe-2026-creative-trends and https://news.adobe.com/news/2026/06/creators-toolkit-report-2026

## Required divergence before selection

- Generate at least three different visual mechanisms, not three cosmetic skins of one layout.
- Name the tension each mechanism makes visible and the product truth that supports it.
- Preserve one specific “weird corner”: a brand-, audience-, transcript-, product-, or market-specific detail that a generic SaaS asset would not contain.
- Select one mechanism using message fit, proof value, channel readability, and brand distinctiveness. Do not average the options together.

## Semantic-specificity gates

Reject the work when any answer is no:

- Would the background still make sense if the headline were removed?
- Does every major animated object represent a real noun, state, comparison, or transition in the message?
- Would swapping in a materially different product leave the composition essentially unchanged?
- Does the visual show product evidence, a mechanism, or a meaningful before/after before the CTA?
- Is there one dominant focal event with supporting motion, rather than several effects competing at once?
- Does the final static frame communicate the same core idea without relying on animation?

## Motion rules

- Animate causality: input to comparison, comparison to finding, finding to action.
- Use productive motion for evidence and expressive motion only for the decisive reveal or primary action.
- Prefer transform and opacity with restrained easing. Ban perpetual floating, decorative particle fields, meaningless orbiting, gratuitous glow, and random parallax.
- Every entrance must originate from a meaningful spatial relationship.
- Hold the complete message long enough for a mobile viewer to read it.

## Visual anti-patterns

- Empty dark gradient plus centered headline and glowing pill button.
- Generic glass cards with no product or message function.
- Abstract dashboards that fabricate product capability.
- Identical composition across unrelated story angles.
- Decoration used to disguise weak hierarchy.
- “Premium” expressed only through black, neon, blur, particles, and oversized type.

## Review protocol

The author proposes; a separate taste pass tries to prove the work is generic. Approval requires a concrete defense of semantic specificity, visible evidence, and channel fit. Human review remains the final creative gate.
